Europe doesn't have a frontier AI lab at American scale. What it has is something stranger and possibly smarter: a four-layer industrial strategy for AI sovereignty, executed across startups, supercomputers, and EU funding programs simultaneously. Here's the playbook, layer by layer — with the honest assessment of where each layer stands.

## Layer 1: Silicon

The least visible layer and the most strategic. **SiPearl** (France) designs the Rhea processors going into EU exascale supercomputers — the continent's bet that it can compute at the frontier on its own chips. Around it, a cluster of inference-chip startups: **Axelera AI** (Netherlands), **SEMRON** (Germany, 3D memcapacitive chips), **VSORA** (France), **Kalray** (France), **GreenWaves** (France, ultra-low-power), **SpiNNcloud** (Germany, neuromorphic supercomputers).

None of these competes with NVIDIA today. That's not the point. The point is optionality: a domestic silicon pipeline that can serve inference — where most AI compute will eventually live — without depending on a single American vendor's allocation queue.

## Layer 2: Clouds

Sovereign models need sovereign GPUs. **Nscale** (UK) raised a $2B Series C at a $14.6B valuation to build AI hyperscale capacity in Europe. **Nebius** (Netherlands) runs full-stack GPU infrastructure. **Northern Data** (Germany), **Berget AI** (Sweden, explicitly sovereign and GDPR-compliant), **Regolo** (Italy, zero-retention inference) fill out a genuinely deep bench.

Behind them sit the generalist sovereign clouds — OVHcloud, Scaleway, Hetzner, STACKIT — all adding GPU offerings. Europe's AI cloud story isn't one moonshot; it's a dozen companies making "your training data never leaves the jurisdiction" a purchasable feature.

## Layer 3: Models

This is the famous layer. **Mistral AI** (France) remains the proof that Europe can train frontier models and release open weights. **Kyutai** (France) does genuinely open research, including the Moshi voice model. **Domyn** (Italy, formerly iGenius) leads the EU's EUROPA consortium for sovereign models. **OpenEuroLLM** — a €37.4M Digital Europe project across 20 partners — is building open multilingual models for all 24 EU languages.

And the cautionary tale: **Aleph Alpha**, Germany's great sovereign-AI hope, signed a definitive merger agreement with Canada's **Cohere** in September 2026. The combined company operates as Cohere with dual headquarters in Toronto and Berlin. Sovereignty by merger is still sovereignty of a kind — but it's a reminder that the model layer consolidates fast, and national champions don't always stay national.

## Layer 4: Applications and tooling

The quietest layer and arguably Europe's strongest. **Qdrant** (Germany) and **Weaviate** (Netherlands) are the two leading open-source vector databases in the world. **Jina AI** and **Mixedbread** (both Berlin) build open embedding models. **Langfuse** (Germany) does LLM observability, **deepset** (Germany) built Haystack, **Giskard** (France) does LLM testing, **LightOn** (France) was Europe's first GenAI IPO. The plumbing of the AI age — the unglamorous infrastructure every app needs — is disproportionately European and disproportionately open source.

## The honest scorecard

Where Europe is genuinely deep: inference silicon, GPU clouds, open models, and AI tooling. Where it's thin: frontier training at American capital scale, and consumer distribution — the chatbots billions of people actually touch.

But here's the thing the scorecard misses: sovereignty was never about winning every layer. It's about having *no layer where you're helpless*. Europe's playbook doesn't need a European OpenAI. It needs European chips that can run open models, on European clouds, under European law, built with European tooling. Layer by layer, that stack now exists. The next five years decide whether it compounds — or gets acquired, layer by layer, like Aleph Alpha.
