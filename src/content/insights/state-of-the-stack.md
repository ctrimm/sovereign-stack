We built this directory to answer one question — *whose laws govern your data?* — product by product. Six weeks of cataloging later, the catalog itself has started answering questions we didn't ask. Here is what 359 listings across 21 categories and 36 jurisdictions actually say.

All numbers below are computed from the live catalog, and they update with every deploy. See them visualized on our [stats page](/stats/).

## 1. The US and Germany are in a dead heat

The two largest domiciles in the directory: **United States, 68 listings. Germany, 67.** One listing apart.

This surprises people who assume a sovereignty directory is a European directory. It isn't — [we've written about why](/insights/why-we-list-us-vendors/). But the near-tie is still telling: Germany alone accounts for nearly a fifth of the catalog, and **55% of all listings are EU-domiciled**. The sovereign tech economy has a clear geographic center of gravity, and it's the EU — with the US as a strong second pole, not an afterthought.

The rest of the top ten: France (43), Switzerland (26), Netherlands (23), UK (18), Sweden (15), Canada (11), India (7), Poland (7). Thirty-six jurisdictions total, from Iceland to South Korea.

## 2. AWS is the incumbent everyone is replacing

The most-replaced product in the entire directory isn't Gmail or Google Analytics. It's **AWS — 39 listings** name it as the thing they replace. Cloud hosting is our largest category by far (64 listings), and the sovereign cloud wave is overwhelmingly a *European* story: German, French, Swedish, Dutch, and Finnish providers selling jurisdiction as a feature.

Gmail is second (19), then OpenAI (14) — the AI displacement is already the third-largest replacement vector in the catalog, which tells you how fast that market moved.

## 3. AI ate the directory

AI tools is our second-largest category at **58 listings** — it didn't exist a month ago. It now spans the full stack: European model labs (Mistral, Poolside, Domyn), open-weight models (Qwen, DeepSeek), EU AI clouds (Nscale, Nebius, Northern Data), European AI silicon (SiPearl, Axelera AI, SEMRON), and open-source tooling (Qdrant, Langfuse, Jina AI).

The interesting split: the *models* conversation is dominated by a few famous labs, but the *infrastructure* conversation — clouds, chips, vector databases — is where Europe is genuinely deep. Sovereignty in AI won't be decided by who trains the best chatbot. It'll be decided by who owns the GPUs and the fabs.

## 4. Open source is a minority — but a strategic one

**38% of listings are open source; 34% are self-hostable.** The sovereign stack, in practice, is mostly *managed services from well-chosen jurisdictions*, not self-hosted software. That's worth saying plainly: for most buyers, sovereignty means picking the right vendor and the right law, not running the server yourself.

But here's the paradox in the data: **US-domiciled listings are 53% open source** — the highest share of any major domicile, ahead of Germany (46%). The country whose law makes its vendors the riskiest choice is also the country producing the most auditable software. Open source is the great equalizer: code you can read and host yourself is sovereign regardless of where the company sits. Domicile matters most exactly where openness is absent.

## 5. Freemium won the pricing war

141 listings are freemium, 97 paid, 69 usage-based, 52 free. The sovereign tech market prices like the mainstream SaaS market — which is good news. It means switching doesn't require a philosophical commitment to paying more; in most categories there's a free tier to try and a paid tier that costs less than the incumbent.

## What the data doesn't show

Every catalog has a bias, and ours is *what we went looking for*. Email is over-represented because we dug deep there; Asia, Africa, and Latin America are under-covered relative to their tech economies; community projects without companies are systematically missing because our schema requires a domicile. We're fixing all three — [submit what we're missing](/submit/).

The numbers will keep moving. That's the point: a directory is a living dataset, and this one now reports on itself. Watch the [stats page](/stats/) — and if the next 359 listings teach us something new, we'll write that up too.
