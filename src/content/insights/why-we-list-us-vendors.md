Roughly a third of this directory is US-domiciled. If you've read our jurisdiction guide, you might be wondering whether we've lost the plot. We haven't. Here's the honest reasoning.

## Sovereignty is relative to the buyer

A German company choosing between a US-domiciled vendor and a Chinese-domiciled vendor is making a sovereignty decision, and the answer isn't automatically "neither." A Brazilian startup might rationally prefer a US vendor over a domestic one if its threat model is local corruption rather than foreign surveillance. An American homelabber's sovereignty project looks different from a French hospital's.

A directory that only listed EU vendors would be a political statement, not a buyer's tool. We catalog the jurisdiction; you supply the threat model.

## The US makes excellent sovereign tools

Some of the best sovereignty-enhancing software in the world is American: **Bitwarden** (open source, self-hostable password management), **Framework** (repairable hardware), **Cal.com** (self-hostable scheduling), **Standard Notes** (encrypted notes). Open source plus self-hosting is a sovereignty technology that works *regardless* of domicile — a vendor that cannot read your data has nothing to hand over, CLOUD Act or not.

Pretending these don't exist would make the directory worse and its readers less sovereign. The honest move is to list them with their domicile clearly marked, so a buyer who needs non-US jurisdiction can filter them out in one click.

## What we don't do

We don't rank US vendors above others. We don't take money for placement — sponsorship buys visibility, never position, and every paid slot is labeled. And we don't launder jurisdiction: if a vendor markets itself as "EU-hosted" while domiciled in Delaware, the listing says Delaware.

## The test

Ask of any directory: does it help you make *your* decision, or does it make the decision for you? Ours gives you domicile, residency, license, and source code status for every product, then gets out of the way. A third of our catalog being American isn't a contradiction of the thesis. It *is* the thesis: sovereignty is relative, so the data must be absolute.
